The Salt
House

By Nora Vance · Directed by Imani Cole

A lighthouse keeper has kept the same light for thirty-one years. The night before the station is decommissioned, her sister arrives with a suitcase and a question neither of them has been willing to ask. A play about inheritance, weather, and the things we keep lit out of habit.

The building

A cinema that forgot
to show films.

Built in 1924, dark by 1979, reopened as a theatre in 1988. The projection booth is now the lighting box.

The Ash Street Picture House opened with 480 seats and a Wurlitzer. By the late seventies the balcony was closed and the roof was leaking, so the last projectionist turned off the carbon arc and locked the door. Nine years later a group of actors persuaded the city to let them put on a play in the lobby.

We took out every second row, widened the stage, and kept the original ceiling roses. The seats are refurbished from the 1962 refit; the bar is in what used to be the ticket booth. On quiet afternoons you can still find the old reel boxes under the stairs.
